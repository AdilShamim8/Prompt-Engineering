# Exam Preparation Prompts

Companion prompts to the main students guide for the exam cycle: planning the run-up, practicing under realistic conditions, diagnosing practice failures, and managing the final 48 hours. Exam prep is a training problem, not a reading problem — these prompts treat it that way.

***

## 1. The Exam Readiness Audit

**Prompt:**
```
My exam: [COURSE/FORMAT — e.g., 3-hour closed-book final]. Date: [DATE]. Topics
and my confidence (1-5): [LIST]. Time available before the exam: [HOURS/WEEKS].
1. Honest verdict: is my current state pass-ready, borderline, or behind —
   based on the confidences I stated?
2. Triage the topics: must-fix (low confidence, high exam weight), should-fix
   (low confidence, low weight), polish (high confidence), ignore (high
   confidence, low weight).
3. Build the study plan: allocate my available hours to the tiers, with the
   largest block on must-fix.
4. The burnout check: does this plan have rest, and would I actually sustain
   it? If not, propose the realistic version.
```

- **Purpose:** A prioritized plan instead of alphabetical panic.
- **Why it works:** The confidence × weight matrix directs hours where they change the outcome most.
- **Use Case:** Mid-semester planning, finals week, professional exams.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the pre-exam checkpoints: at [2 weeks] and [1 week] out, what self-test
score on what question set tells me the plan is working or needs surgery?
```

***

## 2. Practice Exam Simulation

**Prompt:**
```
Create a realistic practice exam for [COURSE]. Format: [LENGTH, question types,
mark allocation]. Coverage: [TOPICS with weights if known].
1. Match the real format exactly — same question types, same marks-per-question
   ratio, same time pressure per question.
2. Include at least 2 questions that require combining two topics.
3. Marking scheme included: what earns and loses points, so I can self-grade
   honestly.
4. Do NOT show answers until I ask — I will attempt everything first, timed.
Afterwards I will paste my answers for grading against your scheme.
```

- **Purpose:** Practicing the exam, not practicing studying.
- **Why it works:** Format-matched, timed simulation trains the actual skill: allocating minutes and performing under pressure.
- **Use Case:** Finals, standardized tests, certification exams.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
After grading my attempt, produce the error-pattern analysis: conceptual gaps
versus careless slips versus time-pressure losses — each needs a different fix.
```

**Limitations & verification:** AI-written questions can drift from your course's actual difficulty and scope — validate against past papers your instructor released, and treat disagreements as calibration data.

***

## 3. Past Paper Error Autopsy

**Prompt:**
```
I did a past paper and got [SCORE]. Here are my errors: [DESCRIBE each — wrong
answer, what I did, what the answer was].
Autopsy each error:
1. Classify: concept gap (didn't know), retrieval failure (knew but couldn't
   recall under pressure), procedure slip (knew the idea, botched execution),
   misread question, or time ran out.
2. For each class: the different remedy — concept gaps get study, retrieval
   gets more practice testing, procedure gets slow careful drills, misreading
   gets a question-reading ritual, time gets pacing strategy.
3. What my error pattern says about how I should spend the remaining [TIME].
```

- **Purpose:** Turning wrong answers into a targeted fix list.
- **Why it works:** The five-way classification prevents the universal (and useless) response to all errors: "study more."
- **Use Case:** After any practice test or mock exam.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Project my current error rate to an exam-day score, and state which single
error class, if eliminated, moves the projection most.
```

***

## 4. The Condensed Knowledge Sheet

**Prompt:**
```
Help me build the one-page sheet I am allowed to take into my open-book exam
(or my final review sheet otherwise) for [COURSE].
1. From my notes: [PASTE KEY NOTES], extract what belongs on ONE page:
   formulas with when-to-use conditions, decision trees for "which method"
   questions, common traps, and unit conversions.
2. Ruthlessly prioritize: what did past papers actually require looking up?
3. Format it so my eye finds things in 2 seconds under pressure — my structure
   preference: [TABLES / LISTS / DIAGRAMS].
4. What to leave OFF: things I will remember anyway; sheet space is for the
   fragile knowledge only.
```

- **Purpose:** A sheet built for retrieval speed, not completeness theater.
- **Why it works:** The constraint of one page forces the prioritization that reveals what you actually use.
- **Use Case:** Open-book exams, final-day review sheets.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Build the sheet in layers: core (always), likely-needed (if space), and a
separate "night before" sheet for pure memorization items.
```

***

## 5. Final 48 Hours Protocol

**Prompt:**
```
It is 48 hours to my exam. State: [READINESS SELF-ASSESMENT], [REMAINING
GAPS], [MY SLEEP SCHEDULE TYPICALLY].
1. What to do and NOT do in the final 48 hours — new material learning is
   mostly off the table; what remains legitimately valuable?
2. Design the schedule: sleep protection, final practice passes, logistics
   prep (materials, route, timing), and deliberate wind-down.
3. The morning-of routine: what to review (and not review) in the last 2 hours.
4. In-exam strategy for my format: [TIME/MARKS], including the first-5-minutes
   plan (read-through, mark easy questions, budget time per mark).
```

- **Purpose:** The window where good decisions about rest and pacing matter more than any remaining study.
- **Why it works:** Protects sleep and pacing — the two highest-leverage, most-sacrificed factors in exam performance.
- **Use Case:** Finals, board exams, bar-style multi-hour tests.

***

## 6. Post-Exam Learning Extraction

**Prompt:**
```
My exam is done. Before I forget: [DESCRIBE what came up, what surprised me,
what I struggled with].
1. Extract the transferable lessons for my NEXT exam: which study methods
   paid off, which did not, what the exam actually rewarded.
2. Update my approach: what should I do differently in [NEXT COURSE/EXAM]?
3. Flag anything I should follow up on for genuine understanding — things I
   memorized but never understood, that my degree program will build on.
4. Close the loop: draft the note-to-future-self to open at the start of the
   next exam cycle.
```

- **Purpose:** Converting each exam into a calibration data point for the next one.
- **Why it works:** Exam insights decay within days; captured immediately, they compound across a degree.
- **Use Case:** After any significant exam, before results even arrive.

***

## Shared Verification Rules for All Exam Prep Prompts

1. **Course materials are the source of truth.** AI-generated practice can drift in scope and difficulty — calibrate every practice set against released past papers and the syllabus.
2. **Practice must be timed to count.** Untimed practice inflates confidence; simulation is the point.
3. **Errors are data, not verdicts.** Every prompt above converts failure into classification — the classification is where improvement actually starts.
4. **Sleep is study infrastructure.** Any plan that trades sleep for cramming is losing on the arithmetic of memory consolidation, whatever it feels like.
