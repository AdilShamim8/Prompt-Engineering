# Onboarding & Offboarding Prompts

Companion prompts to the main HR guide. The main guide covers recruitment rigorously; these prompts cover the equally consequential bookends of the employee lifecycle — bringing people in well and seeing them out well. All the CARE framework principles (Compliance-first, Accessible language, Risk mitigation, Ethical standards) apply throughout, and the human-judgment boundaries from the main guide are absolute here too.

***

## 1. Onboarding Plan Builder

**Prompt:**
```
Act as an HR onboarding specialist. Create a structured onboarding plan for:
[ROLE — level, department]. Company context: [size, remote/hybrid/onsite,
tools they'll need].
Structure the plan across the first 30-60-90 days:
1. Pre-boarding (offer to day 1): paperwork, equipment, access requests,
   welcome touchpoint — with owner assignments per item.
2. Week 1: logistics, introductions, role clarity — what "successful first
   week" looks like, concretely.
3. 30/60/90 milestones: what the new hire should understand, be able to do,
   and be contributing by each checkpoint.
4. The buddy system: what a peer buddy covers that a manager does not.
5. The feedback loops: scheduled check-ins at day 7, 30, 60, 90 — with the
   questions each asks.
6. Compliance items: policy acknowledgments, required trainings — listed
   separately so they are never missed.
```

- **Purpose:** Onboarding as a designed system rather than an improvised first week.
- **Why it works:** Milestone-based structure makes onboarding quality measurable, and separating compliance items ensures regulatory requirements never ride on goodwill.
- **Use Case:** New hires at any level; adapting for internal transfers.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the remote-specific layer: what changes for a remote hire — social
integration, communication norms, timezone handoffs, and the deliberate
replacements for hallway learning.
```

## 2. Role Clarity Document (The 30-Day Confusion Killer)

**Prompt:**
```
Draft a role clarity document for [POSITION] reporting to [MANAGER ROLE].
Sources of truth: [paste job description and any team context].
1. Decision rights: what this role decides alone, decides with approval,
   and must escalate — with the thresholds stated.
2. Success measures: the 3-5 outcomes this role owns at 90 days and at one
   year.
3. Interfaces: which teams/roles this position collaborates with, delivers
   to, and receives from — one line each on what flows across.
4. The boundaries: what this role is NOT responsible for (the classic
   day-30 confusion source).
5. Open questions for the manager to confirm: [list what the sources leave
   ambiguous — do not resolve these yourself].
```

- **Purpose:** The document that prevents the most common early-tenure failure: ambiguity about what the role actually owns.
- **Why it works:** "What this role is NOT responsible for" is the section every job description omits and every new hire needs.
- **Use Case:** Onboarding packets; internal role changes; post-reorg clarity resets.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the first-projects list: 2-3 small, visible wins available in the first
month that build competence and credibility without high risk.
```

## 3. New Hire Check-In Scripts

**Prompt:**
```
Draft the check-in conversation guides for a new hire's first 90 days.
Day 7 (focused on belonging and basics), Day 30 (role clarity and early
friction), Day 60 (progress and integration), Day 90 (consolidation and
forward view).
For each:
1. 5-6 open questions, ordered from easy to substantive
2. What the manager should listen FOR (not just the answers) — warning
   signs of isolation, mismatch, or blocked learning
3. The action rule: what gets captured and committed at the end of each
   conversation
4. What NOT to ask in the first weeks (e.g., premature performance
   pressure, comparisons to predecessors)
```

- **Purpose:** Early-tenure conversations that surface problems while they are still cheap to fix.
- **Why it works:** Staged scripts prevent the default check-in ("How's it going? Great.") that collects no information.
- **Use Case:** Managers of new hires; HR auditing onboarding health.

**Limitations & verification:** Conversations belong to the manager and the human relationship — scripts structure them, never replace them. Notes from these conversations enter HR systems per your data policy, not casual storage.

## 4. Offboarding Checklist & Knowledge Transfer

**Prompt:**
```
Design the offboarding process for a departing [ROLE]. Departure type:
[voluntary/involuntary/retirement — note: for involuntary departures, add
"coordinated with legal and leadership approval" flags throughout].
1. The compliance core: final pay requirements, benefits continuation
   paperwork, system access revocation timeline, asset return — each with
   owner and deadline, separated from everything discretionary.
2. Knowledge transfer plan: the inventory template (what only this person
   knows), the transfer methods (documentation, shadowing, recorded
   walkthroughs), and the realistic prioritization for [NOTICE PERIOD].
3. The communication plan: who learns what, when — team, collaborators,
   external contacts — with message drafts appropriate to each audience.
4. The exit conversation structure: questions that elicit honest insight
   (what to start/stop/continue) without putting the departing employee
   in an awkward position.
5. The alumni relationship: what stays appropriate after departure —
   references, boomerang hiring, professional network.
```

- **Purpose:** Departures handled with operational completeness and human decency.
- **Why it works:** Separating the compliance core from discretionary items guarantees the legal minimums; the knowledge-transfer inventory addresses the organizational amnesia that follows most exits.
- **Use Case:** Resignations, retirements, role transitions.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the single-point-of-failure alert: the offboarding template should flag
when the departing person is the sole holder of critical knowledge — and
the emergency mitigation if notice is short.
```

**Limitations & verification:** Final pay, benefits, and notice requirements are jurisdiction-specific and legally binding — this structure organizes the process; your employment counsel and local regulations define the required contents. Involuntary departures follow your organization's formal process with legal involvement, always.

## 5. Internal Transfer Onboarding

**Prompt:**
```
Adapt onboarding for an internal transfer into [ROLE] from [PREVIOUS ROLE].
1. What transfers know already (company context, tools, culture) — and the
   trap of assuming they know more than they do about THIS function.
2. The identity shift: from respected expert back to novice — what the
   plan does to make that psychologically safe.
3. The relationship reset: new stakeholders to build trust with, and the
   shadow of the old role (people still routing requests to them).
4. The 30/60/90 for transfers: how milestones differ from external hires —
   faster on some dimensions, slower on others.
5. The bridge-back problem: how we prevent the old team from continuing
   to lean on them (with the manager scripts to renegotiate boundaries).
```

- **Purpose:** Internal mobility that succeeds instead of losing a good employee to a clumsy transition.
- **Why it works:** Names the two failure modes unique to transfers — assumed knowledge and the bridge-back drain — that generic onboarding misses entirely.
- **Use Case:** Internal promotions, cross-functional moves, restructuring.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the 6-month retention check: the specific signals that a transfer is
struggling in ways the hiring process missed — and the intervention options
at that point.
```

## 6. Onboarding Experience Audit

**Prompt:**
```
Audit our onboarding from the new hire's experience perspective. Current
process: [DESCRIBE — timeline, materials, touchpoints, tools].
1. The first-day simulation: walk through what a new hire experiences
   hour by hour — where are the dead times, the confusions, the moments
   of feeling unwelcome?
2. The cognitive load audit: how many new systems, names, norms, and
   processes hit them in week 1 — what can be sequenced later?
3. The belonging signals: what in the current design says "we're glad
   you're here" — and what accidentally says "figure it out yourself"?
4. The failure modes: where does onboarding most often break (equipment
   not ready, access delays, absent managers) — and the pre-arrival
   checklist that prevents each.
5. Quick wins: the 3 changes with the highest experience improvement per
   unit of effort.
```

- **Purpose:** Onboarding quality seen from the chair that matters.
- **Why it works:** The hour-by-hour simulation surfaces the gaps that process documents hide — onboarding is experienced in time, not in checklists.
- **Use Case:** Process improvement; preparing for growth hiring; remote-first audits.

## Shared Verification Rules for All Onboarding/Offboarding Prompts

1. **Legal requirements are external.** Final pay, notice, benefits continuation, and data handling follow your jurisdiction and counsel's guidance — AI output organizes, it never defines compliance.
2. **People data stays protected.** Onboarding and offboarding details are personal data — drafts containing real employee information belong in approved HR systems, not general AI tools (see the main guide's privacy rules).
3. **The human moments are the process.** Scripts and checklists structure the experience; the manager's genuine attention delivers it — never send an AI-written welcome or farewell as-is.
4. **Involuntary departures follow formal process.** Legal review, documented justification, and dignity protocols lead; these prompts support the operational layer only.
