# Compensation & Benefits Communication Prompts

Companion prompts to the main HR guide for the most emotionally loaded communications in HR: pay. Compensation work sits at the intersection of money, fairness perceptions, and legal requirements — the prompts below structure the communication craft, with external authority (comp surveys, legal requirements, company policy) explicitly kept in its proper place.

***

## 1. Salary Communication (Offers & Adjustments)

**Prompt:**
```
Draft a salary communication: [TYPE — offer / annual adjustment / role-change
adjustment]. The number: [AMOUNT or BAND]. Context: [the change, the
rationale that can be shared, market context].
1. The draft: number stated plainly and early — burying it in paragraphs
   reads as apology and generates the exact anxiety it avoids.
2. The rationale layer: the factors that drove the number (market range,
   internal level, experience) — stated without promising future
   progression I cannot guarantee.
3. The comparison discipline: nothing that invites unflattering or
   misleading comparisons — no "this is our best offer ever", no
   references to colleagues.
4. The conversation plan: pay numbers are spoken first, written second —
   and always in private, with time to absorb and ask.
5. The anticipated questions: the 3 most likely, each with an honest,
   policy-compliant answer (and "let me confirm that" where honesty
   requires checking rather than guessing).
```

- **Purpose:** Pay communications that are clear, defensible, and humane.
- **Why it works:** Number-first structure and the comparison discipline address the two ways pay letters go wrong: burying the decision, and framing it against references that later look bad.
- **Use Case:** Offer letters; merit cycles; equity/bonus communications.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the total-rewards framing: how to present the full picture (base,
bonus, benefits, development, flexibility) without letting it obscure a
weak base number — transparency, not misdirection.
```

**Limitations & verification:** Pay ranges, pay-equity statements, and transparency obligations are increasingly regulated and vary by jurisdiction — counsel and your comp policy define what must be said; this prompt shapes how clearly it is said.

## 2. Benefits Enrollment Communication

**Prompt:**
```
Design our benefits enrollment communication for [AUDIENCE — all staff /
new hires]. The benefits: [LIST — health, retirement, leave, EAP, others].
Constraints: [ENROLLMENT WINDOW, PLATFORM].
1. The plain-language conversion: each benefit explained in what-it-means-
   for-you terms — one sentence per benefit, jargon decoded (premium,
   deductible, out-of-pocket max in one honest analogy each).
2. The decision checklist: what choices employees actually make, ranked
   by consequence — with the default outcome stated when someone does
   nothing.
3. The life-situation routing: which benefits matter most for common
   situations (new parent, nearing retirement, chronic condition, young
   and healthy) — so people find their own relevant rows first.
4. The question path: where answers come from — and the deadline
   communicated three times, three ways.
5. The accessibility pass: the communication readable at [TARGET READING
   LEVEL], available in [FORMATS], with the numerate-but-newcomer
   assumption (smart people who lack benefits vocabulary — not the
   reverse).
```

- **Purpose:** Benefits communications employees actually use to make good decisions.
- **Why it works:** The life-situation routing converts a benefits catalog into a decision tool — the difference between information sent and decisions made.
- **Use Case:** Annual enrollment; new-hire packets; mid-year qualifying events.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the misunderstanding pre-mortem: the 3 ways employees most commonly
misread this enrollment (based on prior years' questions) — and the
sentence that prevents each.
```

**Limitations & verification:** Benefit plan details are legally precise documents — the plain-language layer links TO the official plan documents, never replaces them. Every specific number (deductibles, matches) is verified against current plan documents before sending.

## 3. Answering the "Am I Paid Fairly?" Conversation

**Prompt:**
```
Prepare me for an employee asking whether they are paid fairly.
What I know: [their band position, the honest market picture, what the
company's comp philosophy says]. What I cannot share: [individual
others' pay, per policy/law in my jurisdiction].
1. The transparent frame: what our comp philosophy IS (how levels and
   ranges work here) — explained without revealing restricted
   information.
2. The honest individual answer: what can be said about where they sit
   in their range and WHY (factors: level, experience, performance,
   market timing) — and what genuinely cannot be said, said as "I can't
   share X, and here is why".
3. The growth path answer: what actually moves comp here — the mechanics,
   not reassurance ("promotions and level changes move range; within-
   range moves follow [PERFORMANCE/MARKET] cycles").
4. The escalation honesty: if the market has genuinely moved beyond our
   bands, saying so (to the extent I can) rather than defending an
   indefensible number.
5. The follow-through I commit to: what I will check and when I will
   come back — pay conversations without follow-through damage trust
   worse than silence.
```

- **Purpose:** Fairness conversations that build trust even when the answer is "not yet."
- **Why it works:** "I can't share X, and here is why" honesty preserves credibility where both fake transparency and stonewalling destroy it.
- **Use Case:** Retention-risk conversations; post-review comp questions; offer negotiations.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the system-feedback layer: when this question recurs from many
employees, it is market data — how I aggregate (anonymized) and route
that signal to comp leadership.
```

**Limitations & verification:** Pay transparency laws differ sharply across jurisdictions (and are changing quickly) — what must, may, and cannot be disclosed is a legal question answered per location. Verify current requirements with counsel before using any script in a new jurisdiction.

## 4. Total Rewards Statements

**Prompt:**
```
Design our total rewards statement. Components to include: [COMP, BONUS,
EQUITY, RETIREMENT MATCH, INSURANCE COSTS COVERED, LEAVE VALUE, PAID
DEVELOPMENT, FLEXIBILITY/REMOTE VALUE IF APPLICABLE].
1. The structure: cash compensation first and unobscured — the rest
   itemized with honest values, clearly labeled as company-paid cost
   (not "your value").
2. The honesty calibration: for each component, is the stated value
   defensible if challenged? Flag any that tempt inflation (assigned
   values for perks are the classic offender).
3. The comparison context: what the statement compares against
   (industry, prior year) — and what it deliberately does not
   (individual peers).
4. The unanswered-question path: the statement will generate questions;
   where they go, answered by whom.
5. The frequency and channel decision: when this lands for maximum
   comprehension rather than maximum guilt (not during crunch, not
   paired with ask-for-more news).
```

- **Purpose:** Transparency that informs rather than spins.
- **Why it works:** The honesty-calibration pass ("defensible if challenged") keeps total rewards statements from becoming the credibility-damaging genre they risk being when perk values get inflated.
- **Use Case:** Annual statements; retention conversations; offer comparisons.

## 5. Pay-Related Change Communication (Market Adjustments, Freezes, Corrections)

**Prompt:**
```
Communicate a difficult pay-related change: [TYPE — salary freeze /
below-expectation cycle / correction of an overpayment / delayed
adjustment]. The facts: [WHAT AND WHY, AS FAR AS SHAREABLE]. The audience
affected: [WHO, HOW MANY].
1. The announcement architecture: decision → reason → duration/scope →
   what happens next → where questions go. No burying, no softening
   that obscures.
2. The empathy-without-pity calibration: acknowledging the impact
   honestly without theatrical apology that implies reversal is
   negotiable.
3. The fairness pre-empt: who is affected and how consistently — because
   the first question everyone asks is "just us?" and the second is
   "did the executives share the pain?"
4. The manager briefing pack: what managers hear BEFORE staff, the FAQ
   they hold, and the questions they must route to HR rather than
   improvise on.
5. The legal review flag: [overpayment recovery, wage-hour implications,
   contract issues] — marked for counsel before anything ships.
```

- **Purpose:** Hard comp news delivered with clarity and retained trust.
- **Why it works:** The fairness pre-empt (executives sharing the pain) addresses the question that actually determines whether freeze communications survive contact with the staffroom.
- **Use Case:** Economic downturns; restructuring; correction of payroll errors.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the re-recruitment risk layer: who is most likely to leave over this
change, what retention conversations happen proactively, and what the
organization is honestly willing to do about it.
```

**Limitations & verification:** Overpayment recovery, wage deductions, and pay-change notices are legally regulated in most jurisdictions — the communication plan comes after the legal path is confirmed, never instead of it.

## 6. Compensation Communication Calendar

**Prompt:**
```
Build the annual compensation communication calendar for our cycle:
[OUR CYCLE — e.g., performance reviews in Q2, comp adjustments announced
Q3, enrollment Q4].
1. The sequence map: which communications must precede which (managers
   before staff, legal review before managers, data verification before
   everything) — with realistic lead times.
2. The tone-tracking: which messages land near each other, and what
   collisions to avoid (a benefits cost increase announced beside a
   weak merit cycle is a resignation trigger — sequence them).
3. The manager enablement: every comp communication gets a manager
   briefing 3-5 days earlier, with the FAQ and the "route to HR" list.
4. The listening layer: where questions and concerns surface after each
   communication — and the review that feeds the next cycle's
   improvements.
```

- **Purpose:** The year of pay communication as a designed experience rather than a series of shocks.
- **Why it works:** Tone-collision tracking (what lands beside what) addresses the comp-communication failures caused by good messages badly sequenced.
- **Use Case:** Annual planning; new HR teams; post-incident calendar repair.

***

## Shared Verification Rules for All Compensation Prompts

1. **The law and the plan documents outrank the prompt.** Pay transparency rules, deduction laws, and plan terms are external, jurisdiction-specific, and changing — counsel and official documents define what is said; these prompts make it clear and humane.
2. **Every number verified against source systems.** Comp communications containing errors (wrong band, stale match percentage) are unrecoverable trust events — verify each figure at final draft.
3. **Anonymize and restrict.** Compensation data is maximally sensitive: these prompts need roles and structures, never identifiable details, and real drafts live in approved systems only.
4. **Spoken first, written second.** Pay numbers and pay decisions are conversations with records — not records without conversations.
