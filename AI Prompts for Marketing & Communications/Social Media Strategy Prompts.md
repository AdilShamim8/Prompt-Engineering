# Social Media Strategy Prompts

Companion prompts to the main marketing guide for the social layer: platform-native strategy, community operations, creator partnerships, and social listening. The theme throughout: social platform mechanics change constantly — these prompts encode the durable strategy layer, and you supply the current platform reality.

***

## 1. Platform Strategy (Native, Not Cross-Posted)

**Prompt:**
```
Design our platform strategy. Our audience: [WHO, WHERE THEY ACTUALLY
SPEND ATTENTION]. Our capacity: [TEAM SIZE, HOURS PER WEEK]. Business
objective: [AWARENESS / COMMUNITY / SUPPORT / DEMAND].
1. The platform selection: 1-2 platforms done natively beat 5 done
   shallowly — with the honest case for each candidate platform's
   fit: audience presence, our content-shaped-ability, and the
   objective it serves.
2. The native adaptation spec: for each chosen platform, what "good"
   looks like THERE — format norms, pacing, tone, and the content
   types we will and will not cross-post (and how anything cross-
   posted gets adapted, not duplicated).
3. The cadence contract: realistic posting rhythm given our capacity —
   with the quality floor: what we skip rather than post badly.
4. The measurement frame: per platform, the ONE metric that matters
   for our objective — and the vanity metrics we explicitly ignore.
```

- **Purpose:** A social presence built for how each platform actually works.
- **Why it works:** The native-adaptation spec and quality floor counteract the two standard failure modes: identical cross-posting (reads as spam everywhere) and cadence goals that force filler.
- **Use Case:** Program launches; channel audits; capacity resets.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the platform-risk note: for each chosen platform, what happens to
our audience if the platform's algorithm or policy shifts — and the
owned-channel hedge (email, community) that reduces dependence.
```

## 2. Content Pillars & Voice System

**Prompt:**
```
Build our content system. Brand voice: [DESCRIBE — or let's define it].
Audience questions: [WHAT THEY ASK / STRUGGLE WITH / FIND FUNNY].
1. The 3-4 content pillars: recurring themes where we have standing
   to speak — each with the audience need it serves and our unique
   angle (the reason it's us saying it).
2. The voice codification: 5 rules with examples — do/say and don't,
   specific enough that two different writers produce the same brand.
   Include the "reads-on-brand / off-brand" test sentences.
3. The format engine: for each pillar, the repeatable formats
   (series, templates, recurring segments) that lower creation cost
   and build recognition.
4. The idea pipeline: how weekly content gets sourced — audience
   questions, internal experts, data moments — so inspiration is a
   system, not a mood.
```

- **Purpose:** Social content production that is systematic without becoming soulless.
- **Why it works:** Voice rules with test sentences and repeatable formats are what allow a brand to scale content without diluting identity.
- **Use Case:** Brand voice definition; onboarding content creators; agency handoffs.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the voice-drift audit: 5 recent posts, scored against the voice
rules — drift is gradual and this is how you catch it quarterly.
```

## 3. Community Operations

**Prompt:**
```
Design our community operations. Community venue: [PLATFORM / GROUP /
COMMENTS]. Current state: [SIZE, ACTIVITY LEVEL, TONE]. Team capacity:
[WHO, HOW MUCH TIME].
1. The moderation framework: what gets removed, hidden, escalated —
   with the escalation path for [SERIOUS ISSUES: threats, self-harm
   signals, legal-adjacent content] going to named owners, not
   community managers making solo judgment calls.
2. The engagement protocol: response time targets by category
   (questions, complaints, praise), the voice for each, and — the
   discipline — what we do NOT engage with (bait, bad-faith
   pile-ons: documented, not just intuited).
3. The cultivation moves: how regulars get recognized, how newcomers
   get welcomed, how we seed discussions rather than broadcast into
   them.
4. The health metrics: contribution ratio (how many members create
   vs. lurk — a rising creator share is the real growth signal),
   response quality, and the sentiment trend.
```

- **Purpose:** A community that runs on systems rather than heroics.
- **Why it works:** The escalation path and documented non-engagement rules protect community managers from the judgment calls they should never make alone.
- **Use Case:** Brand communities; support-focused social; owned groups.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the crisis-interface: how community operations connect to the
crisis communications workflow — community managers are usually the
FIRST to see an emerging crisis, and the handoff protocol is what
makes that visibility an asset instead of a panic.
```

## 4. Creator & Influencer Partnerships

**Prompt:**
```
Design our creator partnership program. Objective: [AWARENESS / CONVERSION
/ CONTENT]. Budget range: [RANGE]. Our niche: [FIELD].
1. The selection criteria: audience-overlap evidence, engagement
   authenticity (comment quality over follower counts), brand-safety
   screen, and the content-native question — can this creator make
   OUR message feel native to THEIR feed?
2. The partnership tiers: [GIFTING / PAID ONE-OFF / AMBASSADOR /
   CO-CREATION] — with realistic expectations, deliverables, and
   what each tier is FOR.
3. The brief architecture: what must be communicated (claims that
   need substantiation, mandatory disclosures per advertising
   regulations) versus where the creator keeps full creative
   control — over-scripted creator content performs like ads
   because it is.
4. The measurement design: how attribution works (honest version —
   codes, tracking links, brand-lift studies) with the expectation-
   setting that creator ROI is directional at small scale.
5. The disclosure compliance note: paid partnerships must be
   disclosed per advertising standards — flagged for the exact
   requirements of our jurisdictions, which are enforced.
```

- **Purpose:** Creator partnerships structured for authenticity, measurement honesty, and legal compliance.
- **Why it works:** The brief-architecture separation (mandatory vs. creator freedom) and the disclosure flag address the two failure modes: content that flops because it's scripted, and enforcement actions because disclosure was sloppy.
- **Use Case:** Program launches; agency coordination; ambassador scaling.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the long-term value case: how ambassador relationships compound
versus one-off posts — and the tracking that shows whether ours are.
```

**Limitations & verification:** Disclosure requirements (FTC and international equivalents) are law, not preference — verify current requirements for your jurisdictions and platforms before campaigns launch.

## 5. Social Listening Program

**Prompt:**
```
Build our social listening practice. What we need to know: [CUSTOMER
SENTIMENT / COMPETITIVE MOVES / CATEGORY TRENDS / CRISIS EARLY
WARNING].
1. The query design: the terms, brand-name variants, misspellings,
   and competitor terms worth tracking — and, equally important,
   the exclusion logic that keeps noise out.
2. The triage protocol: what surfaces daily (questions, complaints,
   crisis signals), weekly (trends, sentiment shifts), monthly
   (competitive, category patterns) — with named owners for each
   cadence.
3. The insight-to-action paths: how a listening finding becomes a
   content decision, a product input, or a support escalation —
   listening without routing is ambient anxiety.
4. The honesty rules: sentiment tools misread sarcasm and context;
   small samples mislead; and the loudest voices are rarely
   representative — the reading discipline that keeps listening
   from becoming hallucination.
```

- **Purpose:** Listening that feeds decisions instead of generating dashboards.
- **Why it works:** The insight-to-action routing and honesty rules address why most listening programs produce data but not change.
- **Use Case:** Program setup; voice-of-customer programs; competitive intelligence.
- **Expert Variation:**
```
Add the blind-spot design: what we CAN'T hear — untagged conversations,
private communities, the customers who never post — and the periodic
research that fills the gap listening never will.
```

## 6. Organic-to-Paid Integration

**Prompt:**
```
Design our organic-to-paid strategy. Current state: [ORGANIC PERFORMANCE,
PAID PROGRAM, BUDGET].
1. The roles: organic's job ([COMMUNITY, TRUST, EDUCATION, COMMERCE-
   CONTENT]) versus paid's job ([REACH BEYOND FOLLOWERS, TARGETING,
   RETARGETING, AMPLIFYING PROVEN ORGANIC WINS]) — and the failure
   mode of judging each by the other's metrics.
2. The amplification pipeline: which organic signals (engagement,
   comments, saves) qualify a post for paid amplification — letting
   the audience pre-test creative before budget touches it.
3. The creative economy: organic as the creative testing ground —
   volume, fast iteration, real feedback — feeding paid's smaller,
   higher-stakes set.
4. The measurement split: how we attribute fairly when the same human
   sees organic and paid — with the incrementality question
   connecting to the Analytics & Testing prompts in this collection.
```

- **Purpose:** Organic and paid as one system with distinct jobs.
- **Why it works:** The qualification pipeline (organic proves, paid amplifies) is the highest-leverage integration and the one most organizations leave to chance.
- **Use Case:** Budget planning; team structure; creative operations.

## Shared Verification Rules for All Social Prompts

1. **Platform mechanics are perishable.** Formats, algorithms, and policies change frequently — validate current platform behavior against official documentation and your own account data before committing strategy to it.
2. **Disclosure and advertising rules are law.** Paid partnerships, sponsored content, and endorsements carry jurisdiction-specific disclosure requirements — verify them, don't improvise them.
3. **Community data is people data.** Listening exports and community member details follow the privacy rules from this repository's other collections — anonymize before prompting, keep exports in approved systems.
4. **Never automate authenticity.** AI drafts and systems support the operation; the account's humanity is the asset — scheduled genericness is how brands quietly lose the attention they paid for.
